# Check the judge and read Calibration

> Check the judge: answer Right or Wrong on its readings of a session. Calibration then shows how often people agree with the judge, question by question.

> **At a glance**
>
> - **Who can:** Anyone who can open the project can give checks: owners, admins, members and viewers. Every role can read the Questions page and Calibration on it. Only owners and admins can edit the questions ([Roles and permissions](/docs/roles-and-permissions)).
> - **Before you start:** Judged sessions. Sequential judges a session about 30 minutes after it ends, if it has at least three meaningful events.
> - **You get:** Calibration: how often people agree with the judge. After 20 checks on the current wording, it says if the judge's confidence holds up. It can name the answer called wrong most often in the last 30 days.
> - **Know this:** From 7 November 2026, rewording one question restarts the agreement counts of that question only. The other questions keep their counts.
> - **Know this:** A change to a question's Name, The summary says, Attention weight (optional) or question order does not restart them.

## Check the judge on a session

A check, a Right or Wrong on one reading of the judge, records whether the judge's reading was right. It never re-judges a session and never changes where a session ranks. [Tune-up](/docs/tune-up) shows your next step to tune the judge.

1. Click **Evidence** in the left rail, or press G, then S.

2. Click the card of the session.

   Result: The session opens in the peek.

3. Click **Open full**, or press O.

   Result: The judge's reading is beside the recording. From 6 November 2026, the readings on the session page have the heading "Was the judge right?". Under them, the page says "Your answer measures the judge. It never changes this reading."

4. Watch the session to decide if each reading was right.

5. Click **Right** or **Wrong** beside the reading.

   Result: The page says, for example, "Saved: “Worth watching” was right."

Or select a reading, then press E for Right or X for Wrong.

| Where | What you can check |
|---|---|
| The session page | Each reading: Came to, Outcome, Friction, each signal that is on, and Worth watching or Not worth watching. From 9 November 2026, also each signal that is unclear, and each moment that the judge found |
| The session in the peek | Came to and Outcome |
| Workbench › Sessions | Came to, else Outcome (column: The judge’s reading) |
| Ask | Each session of an answer: "A match for your question?", with Right and Wrong. It counts in your score (your own count, on the session page), not in Calibration |
| Evidence cards | Nothing |

From 9 November 2026, a moment on the session page reads "Moment", then "Struggled", "Gave up" or "Clear what they came to do". A moment of a question without a role shows the name of the question. For a signal or a moment, Right means that it happened. A moment is Right if it happened anywhere in the session, also at a different point than the one that the judge found. Under each moment, the page shows "Right if it happened anywhere in the session." Wrong means that the moment did not happen in this session. An agent also checks only whether a moment happened. The check prompt tells the agent to give, in its reason, the event where the moment really happened. When an agent checked one of these readings, its check shows under that reading.

### What happens when you click Right or Wrong

- If you click the other answer, your latest check replaces the earlier one.
- In the session in the peek, the buttons are small icons, with a note beside them.
- Beside each reading, a word says how sure the judge was, from "unsure" to "almost certain". Hover over it to see the percent. Calibration compares your checks with the stated likelihood.
- A reading made under an earlier wording, and a reading from Ask, get no line on how often people agree with the judge.
- Under the readings, the link "Calibration: how often people agree with the judge" opens that section of the Questions page. From 6 November 2026, the link shows also before your first check.
- From 8 November 2026, after you click Wrong, a box asks "What did the judge miss? (optional)". Write up to 300 characters and click Save, or click Not now. Right and Wrong wait while the reason saves.
- Above the box, the page says "Your team sees this reason with your name." Your team sees your reason with your name. Agents and AI never read it.
- When the reason is saved, the page says "Your reason is saved with your check." When Sequential cannot save it, the page says "Your check is saved. The reason is not: remove links, emails and secrets, or try again later." Your check counts in both cases.
- Under your own check, the page then shows your reason, for example "You said: “It missed the retry”".

### Call it first

From 8 November 2026, the queue of "Check 5 sessions" has "Call it first". On a session in the queue, the page asks you one question before it shows the judge's reading. You give your answer first. Then the page shows the answer of the judge.

Call it first is on for everyone in the Check 5 sessions queue. To turn it off, use the Call it first switch in the checking bar above the replay. The choice is kept in this browser.

- In the place of the judge's reading, a box with the heading "Call it first" shows the question in the words of your project. Under the question are the answers of that question, and "Can’t tell". Under them, the box says "The judge’s reading shows after you answer."
- Click your answer. Or press A, S, D or F for the first four answers. These keys work while the box has the focus. The box gets the focus when it opens.
- Click "Can’t tell" if you cannot decide. The page then shows the judge's reading. Sequential saves nothing.
- The question is about Came to, Outcome, Friction or Worth watching: the reading that the judge was least sure of. Call it first never asks about a signal or a moment.
- Until you answer, the page does not show the judge's reading. This includes the lane pill, the verdict, the readings, the button Judge this recording now or Judge again, the judge's moments and their marks on the timeline of the player. At the end of the replay, the player does not ask "Was the judge right?". Around this session shows this visit without its pill and its short verdict.
- The facts from the recorder stay on the page: What went wrong, and the marks of errors and failed requests on the timeline.
- After you answer, the page shows all of the reading. The bar says, for example, "The judge said “Yes”. You agree." or "The judge said “Yes”. You disagree." The answer of the judge is in the place of Yes.
- When your answer is the answer of the judge, Sequential saves a Right check. When it is not, Sequential saves a Wrong check. The bar then adds "Say what it missed below (optional).", and the box "What did the judge miss? (optional)" opens under that reading.
- Sequential saves the check with a flag that says that you answered before you saw the reading. Calibration counts these checks the same way as other checks.
- If Sequential cannot save your answer, the box stays and says why.
- Call it first asks only on a session that you did not check before. After the page shows the reading of a session, it does not hide it again. This is also true after Back, after a reload, and when you go back to that session in the same browser tab.
- If Sequential cannot read your checks or the questions, the page shows the reading at once. It does the same for a session without a reading, and for a reading made under an earlier wording.
- When you turn the switch off, the page shows the reading at once. When you turn it on, Call it first starts on the next session.
- Outside the queue, Right and Wrong work as before.

### What a check stores, and which readings get checks

- Sequential stores each check with the answer that the judge showed, the stated likelihood and the wording that the judge used.
- Every reading on the session page gets checks, so each question that people check has evidence.
- In Ask, Sequential stores the check on a session of an answer with the first 200 characters of your search.

## Read Calibration

1. In the left rail, click **Questions**.

2. Open **Calibration**, under the questions.

   Result: From 6 November 2026, the section has this name, and under it the line "How often people agree with the judge, question by question."

Result: A question gets a card when it has at least one check in the last 90 days. A card's count line says, for example, "People agreed with 41 of the 52 readings they checked (78.8%)."

### Why the numbers differ

Three views read the checks:

| View | Where | Whose checks | Covers |
|---|---|---|---|
| Your score | The session page, under the readings: "You have checked 12 readings in this project and agreed with 9." | Yours | The current wording of each question, and your Ask matches |
| Wrong answers | A card of Calibration: "In the last 30 days, people called “Abandoned” wrong 4 of 9 times." | Everyone's | The last 30 days |
| Agreement | Calibration, on the Questions page | Everyone's | The last 90 days |

Your score is a count, not a calibration measure.

### The Calibration tile on the Brief

From 6 November 2026, the Brief can show a Calibration tile.

- With fewer than 20 checks, it says, for example, "12 of 20 checks" and "At 20, Sequential can say how far to trust the judge." From 20 checks, it says, for example, "85.4% right" and "41 of 48 checks". The rate counts only people's checks under the current wording.
- When the judge is surer than it turns out to be right on a question, a line on the tile says, for example, "Friction: the judge is surer than it turns out to be right." Agents' checks show apart, for example "Agents: 20 checks, counted apart."
- On the tile: Check 5 sessions, Ask your agent, and a link, See it by question, that opens Calibration on the Questions page.
- Sequential adds this tile once to every Brief that existed before, as its first tile, the next time the Brief opens while background judging is set up. If you remove it, Sequential does not add it again. A new Brief gets it while people have made fewer than 20 checks and the judge read sessions in the last 7 days.

### See if the judge's confidence holds up

A card says whether the judge's confidence holds up only after 20 checks on the current wording, in ranges with at least 5 checks each. Before that, it says "Too few checks yet to say whether its confidence holds up." After that, it says one of these:

- "Its confidence holds up: it is about as often right as it says."
- "It is surer than it turns out to be right. Read its confident answers with care, or sharpen the question."
- "It is right more often than it says."

From 7 November 2026, a question with 20 checks or more, but too few in each range, says "The checks are spread too thin over its confidence levels to say whether its confidence holds up."

### The chart and earlier wordings

- When at least 5 checks fall where the judge was 70 % sure or more, the card also says how often people agreed then.
- Chart: across, how sure the judge said it was; up, how often people agreed. On the dashed line, the judge is as often right as it says it is sure. A range with fewer than 5 checks shows a hollow dot.
- As a table: it shows the checks per range.
- Earlier wordings: each earlier wording of a question has its own line with its own counts, for example "Under an earlier wording: 12 checks, 75% agreed. Kept apart from today’s." The page never pools earlier wordings into the current wording's figures.

### Read the answers people called wrong

A card can name the answer that people called wrong most often in the last 30 days. For example: "In the last 30 days, people called “Abandoned” wrong 4 of 9 times." The line only reports. It has no controls.

### What the page shows at each count

For each question, the section shows: when it said 90 %, was it right about 90 % of the time on your sessions, with your current wording?

Sequential sets these numbers: 20 checks before a card judges the confidence, 5 checks for a range to count, and a difference of more than 10 points between what the judge said and how often people agreed.

| What the page has | What you see |
|---|---|
| No checks | "Nobody has said yet whether the judge’s readings were right. Open a session and answer “Was the judge right?”; how often people agree shows here, question by question." |
| Fewer than 20 checks in ranges of 5 or more, on the current wording | The count line, and "Too few checks yet to say whether its confidence holds up." |
| 20 checks or more in such ranges | One of the three sentences in [See if the judge's confidence holds up](#see-if-the-judge-s-confidence-holds-up) |

Each of the three sentences compares the stated likelihood with how often people agreed.

The line on each question, on the Questions page:

- From 6 November 2026, each question on the Questions page shows how often people agree with the judge on it, for example "80.9% right · 467 checks". The line opens Calibration.
- From 7 November 2026, each question also has a chip that opens Calibration. It says "Untested" with no checks, for example "9 of 20 checks" with fewer than 20, "Calibrated" when the judge's confidence holds up or it is right more often than it says, "Surer than right" when it is surer than it turns out to be right, and for example "24 checks, too spread to tell". The line "80.9% right · 467 checks" shows only from 20 checks.
- When a question becomes calibrated, the Questions page says so once in each browser, for example "Goal is calibrated: right on 21 of 25 checks (84%)." It says this again after the question is reworded and calibrated again. The first time a browser opens the project's Questions page, it says nothing about questions that are already calibrated. It says at most one such message each time the page loads.
- When the judge is surer than it turns out to be right on a question, the question's card also says, for example, "People called “High” wrong 11 of 33 times." Else it says "It is surer than it turns out to be right."
- Under that, the question's card has:
  - Ask an agent to propose
  - Change the question, for people who may change the questions

The line about wrong answers:

- Under the questions, Calibration shows a card for each question that people checked.
- The line names an answer in the question's own words. For a yes-or-no question, it says "Yes" or "No".
- A card shows the line only when the question has a check under the current wording.
- The line counts every wording in its 30 days. So it can reflect a replaced wording.

## Improve a question

Act when a question is over-confident or under-confident repeatedly, not after one wrong session. A line about wrong answers is a reason to look, not proof.

The usual cause of a bad result is wording that asks for something the judge cannot see. Give the judge evidence that it can read:

- Name your milestones with [custom events](/docs/custom-events).
- If your questions ask about errors, turn on [DevTools capture](/docs/devtools).

> **A new wording restarts the counts of that question**
>
> From 7 November 2026, Sequential tracks the wording of each question on its own. If you reword one question, the agreement counts of that question restart, and your score no longer counts your checks of its earlier wording. The other questions keep their counts. An update to a built-in question does the same on a project that has not customised it. Each earlier wording of a question has its own line with its own counts.

1. Wait until the card of the current wording says whether its confidence holds up.

2. Test the question with [the three tests](#the-three-tests-and-try-on-a-session) in [Questions](/docs/rubric).

3. If the question asks about a cause, name [the evidence for that cause](#why-name-the-evidence-in-a-question).

4. Optional, for owners and admins: click **Edit questions**, then test the draft in **Try on a session**.

   Result: Each try spends from a daily allowance.

5. Click **Save**.

   Result: The change applies to sessions judged from now on.

6. Keep checking readings on session pages. Compare the new wording's card with [the earlier wording's line](#the-chart-and-earlier-wordings).

### What starts a new wording

Sequential links every judgment and every check to the exact wording that the judge read. From 7 November 2026, each question has its own wording, and a check counts toward the wording of its own question.

A change to anything the judge reads of a question starts a new wording for that question only:

- its text or criteria
- its options or levels
- its option keys

Adding, removing or turning off a question does not change the wording of the other questions. If you turn a question off and on again with no change, it keeps its counts.

The judge never sees these, so a change to them keeps the current wording:

- the Name of a question
- The summary says
- Attention weight (optional)
- question order

There is one exception. If Pick it when is blank for an answer, Sequential sends that answer's name to the judge. Sequential always sends a level's name.

A change to Attention weight (optional) does change which sessions need a look on Evidence. So it changes which sessions people check.

### The three tests and Try on a session

- To fix a bad result, reword the question.
- The three tests in [Questions](/docs/rubric):
  - Can the judge answer it from the session?
  - Does it ask one thing?
  - Does it ask about something the judge can observe, not infer?
- The advice in step 3 of Improve a question applies to every question.
- In Try on a session, pick a session, then click Try it.
- To test a new wording quickly, use Try on a session on sessions whose outcome you already know.

## FAQ

### What are checks for?

Sequential judges each session with enough activity against your project's questions: the list on the Questions page, in the left rail. Checks show if those judgments are right on your sessions, and which questions need new wording. They answer questions such as these:

- "The judge said this session was worth watching. Was it?"
- "How often does the team disagree with the judge's Worth watching reading?"
- "When a question says 80 %, is it right about 80 % of the time on our sessions, with our wording?"
- "We reworded a question last month. Is the new wording better calibrated than the old one?"

### Why can't I check a reading?

A signal that is off, and a moment that the judge did not find, have no reading. From 9 November 2026, a signal that the judge leans toward, but is not sure of, has Right and Wrong, as a signal that is on. A session with fewer than three meaningful events gets no readings and no checks. If Sequential no longer has the wording that the judge read, the page says "The questions changed since this session was read, so this reading can no longer be checked."

For the places that have Right and Wrong, see the table in [Check the judge on a session](#check-the-judge-on-a-session).

### What happens to sessions judged before a save?

- Sessions already judged keep their stored judgment. Their session page says "The questions changed since; this reading is from the earlier version."
- Their readings, and Right and Wrong on them, keep the earlier judgment.
- After you reword a question, its readings judged before the save show neither Right nor Wrong as chosen, even if you checked them. Sequential keeps your check under the earlier wording. Readings of the other questions keep your choice.
- If you click Right or Wrong on such a reading, your score changes on screen. When you reload the page, the change is gone.
- The reworded question's agreement counts start again. Its old wording gets its own line. The other questions keep their counts.
- A check of the reworded question on a session judged before the save counts toward its earlier wording. So the new wording's checks come from sessions judged after the save.

### Can I change a question's name and keep the counts?

If only the text on screen reads badly, edit the question's Name or The summary says. Neither starts a new wording, so Sequential keeps the counts. Answer and level names are different: see [the list "What starts a new wording"](#what-starts-a-new-wording).

### Why name the evidence in a question?

The judge reads your labelled events, a few session facts and, when they were measured, error counts. It never sees the replay.

This example is from Sequential's own testing. The first built-in "Hit an error" question asked: "Judging from `events`, does a software error stop the user from completing an action?"

On a session with rage clicks and no error data, the judge, under that wording, leaned clearly towards yes. It reasoned from behaviour to a cause it could not see.

The current wording adds "and the session's error counts (…; absent when not measured)". With that, the same session comes back as can't-tell.

## Limits

- You cannot clear a check. A second click on the same answer does nothing.
- Sequential does not judge sessions again after a change to the questions.
- You cannot change [the numbers that Calibration uses](#what-the-page-shows-at-each-count) for your project.
